## Program Overview
DARPA announced awards to seven teams developing gene editing safety tools and knowledge. The program invests $65 million over four years to understand gene editing technologies, ensure responsible innovation, and address misuse concerns.

## Funding & Timeline
- Total investment: $65 million
- Duration: Four years
- Announcement date: July 19, 2017

## Performing Institutions & Principal Investigators

1. **Broad Institute/Brigham and Women's Hospital/Harvard Medical School** - Dr. Amit Choudhary: Developing on/off switches for genome editing in bacteria, mammals, and insects; creating synthetic editors and chemical inhibitors.

2. **Harvard Medical School** - Dr. George Church: Building systems to detect, prevent, and reverse radiation-induced mutations; developing computational tools and drug screening methods.

3. **Massachusetts General Hospital** - Dr. Keith Joung: Creating sensitive methods to control and measure genome editing activity; developing drug-regulated editors and mosquito gene drive controls.

4. **MIT** - Dr. Kevin Esvelt: Developing "daisy drive" platforms with reversible, geographically-limited gene edits; testing in nematodes and mosquito species.

5. **North Carolina State University** - Dr. John Godwin: Creating mammalian gene drive systems in rodents for invasive species management.

6. **UC Berkeley** - Dr. Jennifer Doudna: Investigating safe gene editing tools against Zika and Ebola; identifying anti-CRISPR proteins.

7. **UC Riverside** - Dr. Omar Akbari: Developing reversible gene drives for Aedes aegypti mosquito control with temporal and environmental safeguards.

## Program Manager
Renee Wegrzyn emphasized that "safety first" guides the full range of applications, providing tools to prevent and mitigate misuse.
