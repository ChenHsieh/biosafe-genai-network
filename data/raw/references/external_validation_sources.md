## Source 1 — CSIS: Opportunities to Strengthen U.S. Biosecurity from AI-Enabled Bioterrorism

- **Citation**: Center for Strategic & International Studies (CSIS), 2024
- **URL**: https://www.csis.org/analysis/opportunities-strengthen-us-biosecurity-ai-enabled-bioterrorism-what-policymakers-should
- **Type**: Policy brief / think tank
- **Key findings**:
  - <3% of 370+ biological AI models have any biosecurity safeguards
  - FY2026 proposed NIST budget cut of ~$325M threatens biosecurity standards work
  - Calls for mandatory evaluation requirements before model deployment
  - Highlights gap between AI capability advances and governance infrastructure
- **Atlas relevance**: Confirms insight on evaluation gaps; supports "evaluator community is small relative to scope"

## Source 2 — PMC: Dual-Use Capabilities of Concern of Biological AI Models

- **Citation**: Gopal et al., 2024. *PLOS Computational Biology* / PMC
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12061118/
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed research
- **Key findings**:
  - Only 1 dual-use capability "fully realized and scientifically validated": optimization of viral serotypes to evade immunity
  - 58% of publications involve US-based authors; 83% from US, UK, China, Germany combined
  - No UK or US government standardized biosecurity evaluation methods released (as of mid-2024)
  - Proposed framework: capability-based (not pathogen-list-based) evaluations with pre-specified thresholds
  - Identified categories: (1) general dual-use capabilities, (2) pandemic-potential pathogen capabilities
- **Atlas relevance**: Confirms US/UK geographic concentration; validates evaluation framework gaps; grounds several policy and AI safety audience insights

## Source 4 — PMC: Responsible AI in Biotechnology (2025)

- **Citation**: *PMC / EMBO Reports*, 2025
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11835847/

- **Key findings**:

- **Atlas relevance**: Confirms evaluation inconsistency; relevant to practitioner audience insights

## Source 5 — RAND: Global Risk Index for AI-Enabled Biological Tools

- **Citation**: RAND Corporation, 2024
- **URL**: https://www.rand.org/
- **Type**: Risk assessment / index
- **Key findings**:
  - Assessed 57 AI-enabled biological tools across 24 countries
  - 13 tools rated "Red" (highest risk), 15 rated "Amber"
  - Tools developed across diverse geographic distribution, most under-governed
  - Highlights need for international coordination on AI bio tools
- **Atlas relevance**: Confirms atlas captures only a fraction of risk landscape; validates Global South gap; grounds "57 tools vs. atlas coverage" comparison

## Source 6 — Open Philanthropy: 2024 Progress Report

- **Citation**: Open Philanthropy / Coefficient, Annual Report 2024
- **URL**: https://www.openphilanthropy.org/
- **Type**: Funder report
- **Key findings**:
  - OP provided ~$63.6M to AI safety work in 2024 (~60% of all tracked AI safety philanthropic funding per LessWrong analysis)

  - $543M tracked in atlas from Coefficient/OP grants across biosecurity, pandemic prep, health R&D

## Source 7 — WMDP / Benchmark Landscape Analysis

- **Citation**: Li et al., WMDP (2024); Epoch AI meta-analysis; VCT comparison papers
- **URL**: https://wmdp.ai/; https://epochai.org/
- **Type**: Research papers / benchmarks
- **Key findings**:
  - WMDP benchmark rapidly saturating; top models approaching ceiling
  - VCT (Virology Capabilities Test) more informative for biosecurity evaluation
  - LAB-Bench, ABC-Bench, BioWeapons Eval all assess overlapping but non-interoperable domains
  - Benchmark fragmentation makes cross-study comparisons difficult
- **Atlas relevance**: Confirms "benchmark fragmentation" insight for AI safety audience; grounds Sprint 13 deep-dive proposal

## Source 8 — NTI: Global South AI-Biosecurity Governance

- **Citation**: Nuclear Threat Initiative (NTI), AIxBio Forum, 2024
- **URL**: https://www.nti.org/
- **Type**: Policy organization report
- **Key findings**:
  - Only 27% of Global South countries have any national AI strategy
  - None of those strategies address biosecurity implications
  - Existing governance frameworks are heavily US/UK/EU centric
  - Pandemic risk disproportionately falls on Global South countries
- **Atlas relevance**: Directly confirms "Global South absent from atlas" as a real gap, not just a sampling artifact

## Source 9 — Singularity Hub / Science: AI Protein & DNA Design Biosecurity

- **Citation**: Multiple sources: Science (ado1671, 2024); Nature Biotechnology (2025); Singularity Hub synthesis (Jan 2026)
- **URL**: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ado1671; https://singularityhub.com/2026/01/02/ai-can-now-design-proteins-and-dna-scientists-warn-we-need-biosecurity-rules-before-its-too-late/
- **Type**: Research article + science journalism synthesis
- **Key findings**:
  - AI can now design novel proteins that retain toxicity and evade current biosecurity screening tools
  - Baker & Church (2024) proposed DNA barcoding of designer proteins for audit trails
  - Proactive regulatory frameworks needed before widespread deployment
  - Calls for international standards on nucleic acid synthesis screening for AI-designed sequences
- **Atlas relevance**: Confirms protein design safety is major white space in atlas; supports Sprint 16 protein design deep-dive

## Source 10 — PMC/EMBO: Security Challenges by AI-Assisted Protein Design

- **Citation**: EMBO Reports, 2024. "Security challenges by AI-assisted protein design"
- **URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11094011/
- **Type**: Peer-reviewed research
- **Key findings**:
  - AI protein design tools can generate variants that evade biosecurity databases used by nucleic acid synthesis providers
  - Demonstrated: toxicity-retaining proteins generated by popular AI design models
  - Current screening tools are database-matching based — structurally inadequate for AI-designed sequences
  - Authors call for updated screening infrastructure and regulation of AI design tools
- **Atlas relevance**: Confirms protein design is the highest-risk white space in atlas; grounds "biggest white space" insight for practitioners
